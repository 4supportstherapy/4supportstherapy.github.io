CAFT Model: Building Marriages That Build Society

Marriage is a key relationship in life. According to Ekstrand (2020), a good marital relationship creates a happy home, which translates to healthy children — and becomes a solid foundation for any society.

On the other hand, a marriage riddled with violence and bitterness leaves scars not only on the couple but also on their children. This can lead to emotional pain passed down through generations.

The most important point: Good marriages do not just happen. They are the result of intentional work by both partners.

Because challenges in marriage are normal, couples benefit from both premarital and ongoing marital therapy. This helps them navigate difficulties with skill and grace. Effective therapy needs a strong theoretical foundation.

4

The CAFT Model Integrates 4 Critical Keys:

Each key is assessed in reference to whole family dynamics

01

1. Personality differences - How your unique traits impact the relationship

02

2. Intrapersonal conflicts - Personal struggles each partner brings

03

3. Interspousal conflict management styles - How you handle disagreements together

04

4. Family of origin influences - Patterns learned from childhood homes

While these factors are individually recognized as key to marital happiness, CAFT brings them together in one unified theory. This model adds to the ever-growing body of knowledge in Marriage and Family Therapy.

CAFT Provides Complete Guidelines:

1. Concept of family dysfunction - Understanding how problems develop

2. Goals of therapy - Defining what healing and success look like

3. Therapeutic techniques - Specific methods we use to create change

4. Role of the therapist - How I guide and support your process

This model is designed for use with couples AND individual family members, always in reference to whole family dynamics. CAFT remains open to constructive critique and further research as it grows.
